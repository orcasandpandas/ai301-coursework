## Who I am in threads

<!-- 2-3 lines. Who is talking when you comment on an issue: your

I am a developer who is relatively new to open-source development and has experience in python along with Java.  
I would love to contribute to a real application that people put into use in real situations and have great respect for the FOSS movement as I admire the openness and transparency it provides.
While I do feel AI is a useful tool for filling in gaps in information and expediting work, I acknowledge that what is being altered must be fully understood first before I make any additions, and any code I add will be vetted first to ensure no changes are blindly added.

### Rule: Reasons for changes
Whenever I make a change, I will be sure to make sure that I specify why that change is being made and how it benefits the repository, rather than using a blank comment.

Wrong: Altered lines 4 and 7, changed the processor() function
Right: Reformatted line 5 comments to be clearer, changed the processor() function so its line were neater for better legibility.

### Rule: No acronyms
In my comments, I will be sure not to overuse acronyms or abbreviations to ensure that new contributors are not confused.  Any project specific acronyms will be fully shown and may also have a brief explanation.
Wrong: Felt that LOTD was not functioning correctly, so I altered lines 4 which were causing the strange output.
Right: I saw that the Linear Orthographic Projection function was missing the feature to rotate the object, so I added a new feature to alter the rotations by 90 degrees for better clarity.

### Rule: Complete sentences
Whenever I make a comment or ask for updates, I will do my best to ensure fully formed sentences are used that detail my reasions for the comment along with what I am doing.
Wrong: Can I work on this?
Right: Hello, I'd like to work on this issue for 1.20.0 of the software, I'll be writing a repro report with my environment, steps, and a log, before attempting to fix the issue.

## Things I never post

<!-- A short list. Promises you cannot keep, tones you refuse,
shortcuts you know you reach for when tired. The skill quotes this
list back at you when a draft crosses it. -->
I will be sure to not rely on Github's generated comments without proofreading, as this could make my contributions confusing.
I will not write unrealistic promises such as "I can do this in X days", and if I feel an issue is too much, I will make this clear as soon as possible.
Ensuring a concise tone I feel is vital; while it doesn't have to be completely professional, I will avoid using words such as "LOL" and I will not speak in a tone that seems overly pressuring.
